# Landing principles — selective decision lenses

Paraphrased and adapted from the existing Marc-principles reference; numbers
preserve that reference's mapping, not a fresh verification of the original post.
Choose the few lenses that answer the current visitor question. These sections
are independent options, not a mandatory page sequence or pass/fail score.

## Positioning and the opening view

| Lens | Landing decision | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| 7 · Familiar headline | Name a recognizable job and useful outcome in the visitor's language. | No jargon replacement with a vague emotional slogan. |
| 9, 11, 30 · Ownable, focused promise | Connect the core job to this product's actual mechanism; keep the opening explanation short enough to repeat. | No forced word count or removal of essential qualifications. |
| 18, 21, 24 · Human stakes | Reflect the user's situation, desired progress and cost of the current workflow. | No invented anxiety, shame, urgency or guaranteed life/career outcome. |
| 20 · Self-sufficient opening | Establish product, audience, value, credible demonstration and next step. | Comprehension matters more than cramming everything above a fixed fold. |
| 17 · Recall | When recall is genuinely uncertain, compare distinct messages with real readers. | Internal preference or an agent's opinion is not a recall test. |

## Product proof and benefits

| Lens | Landing decision | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| 10, 25 · Show the mechanism | Place a real result, readable product example or available trial near the promise. | A fictional example is labeled; do not expose paid access or imply a trial that does not exist. |
| 3, 26 · Specificity | Prefer verified quantities, scoped outcomes and literal limits. If numbers are unavailable, explain the mechanism. | No invented time savings, customer counts or precision. |
| 14, 29 · Relevant proof | Use consented customer language, attributable outcomes and proof for the visitor's actual objection. | Pre-launch: product evidence can replace social proof; beta feedback is labeled, not disguised as paid-customer results. |
| 31 · Switching value | Compare meaningful, verifiable dimensions against the current workaround or an alternative. | Preserve important tradeoffs; request sources for comparative claims. |
| 15, 19 · Credibility and novelty | Founder context or a distinctive demonstration can help when it resolves a real trust or understanding gap. | Neither a founder story nor novelty is mandatory. |

## Page sequence and action

| Lens | Landing decision | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| 6 · One clear idea | Group related information and reveal secondary detail at the appropriate decision point. | A section is not required for every feature. Do not hide material terms. |
| 2 · Attention hierarchy | Use the host DS's approved hierarchy and semantic colors to guide attention. | No new palette or loss of error/success/focus contrast. |
| 22 · Dominant action | Keep the primary decision consistent; repeat the same action where useful on a longer page. | Multiple buttons are not inherently competing; preserve login, help and legitimate alternatives. |
| 28 · Honest CTA | Describe the immediate next step and keep the destination consistent with the promise. | Account creation, payment or required setup is not a completed user outcome. |
| 16 · Findable offer | Make approved price and material conditions accessible while the visitor evaluates fit. | Do not bury billing cadence, eligibility or paid requirements. |

## Conditional distribution and offer questions

- **4, 5 · Sharing:** when sharing or social previews are in scope, show a legible,
  truthful preview and an optional useful share action. No forced sharing or
  exposure of private customer results.
- **12 · Packages:** make existing tiers comparable. Do not manufacture three
  plans because a heuristic mentions three choices; a single clear offer may fit.
- **1, 8, 27, 32 · Commercial strategy:** free access, paid boundaries, payment
  cadence and premium positioning need an explicitly scoped product decision and
  supporting evidence. A landing task only presents the approved model.
- **13, 23 · Trends and naming:** use relevant demand language and the approved
  product name. Repositioning or renaming is a separate assignment.

## Small example — labeled fictional brief

A pre-launch invoice-checking SaaS can show one synthetic invoice with a
duplicate highlighted and explain the review step. It cannot substitute
"10,000 teams save 8 hours a week" for missing evidence. If the actual action
opens a waitlist, use "Join the waitlist", not "Fix my invoices now". No price,
trial or integration is inferred from this example.
