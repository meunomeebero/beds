# Marc Lou Landing Page

Make the right visitor understand the product, see credible value and choose an
appropriate next step. Conversion is an outcome to measure, not a promise this
skill can make. This is a BEDS-authored adaptation, not an official Marc Lou skill.

## Establish the decision

Use the supplied brief and existing product evidence to identify:

- The visitor, arrival context and problem they recognize.
- The product's actual mechanism and the first useful outcome it can deliver.
- The approved offer, available proof and destination of the primary action.

Reuse known facts. If context is incomplete, label assumptions; ask only when a
missing fact materially changes the promise, offer or destination. Do not invent
business policy to complete a layout. A scoped review is findings only; a build
request permits the requested implementation, not a broader funnel rebuild.

## Apply the relevant principles

Read the relevant sections of [Landing principles](references/landing-principles.md).
They are candidate lenses, not a requirement to include every section on a page.

1. Make the opening answer: what is this, who is it for, what changes for me and
   what happens next? Lead with the visitor's job, not the feature inventory.
2. Put a real result, understandable product example or short workflow near the
   promise. Label demonstrations and synthetic data. Without customer proof,
   demonstrate the mechanism; do not manufacture popularity or outcomes.
3. Sequence only the benefits, evidence, objections and terms this visitor needs.
   A bento, pricing grid, FAQ, founder note or testimonial strip is optional, not
   a default SaaS template. Preserve useful completed work.
4. Keep one dominant next decision at a time. Repeating the same CTA down a long
   page is fine; login, navigation, help and an honest secondary path can remain.
   The label must describe the actual next step, not a future result delivered
   only after undisclosed payment or setup.
5. Challenge generic language with the product's own mechanism and customer
   vocabulary. Use verified specificity; otherwise explain how it works rather
   than replacing an adjective with an invented number.

## Respect the system and the visitor

- Precedence: truth, safety, security and accessibility → explicit product/DS
  contracts → observed evidence → user clarity and autonomy → these heuristics.
- In BEDS, reuse public components for their individual jobs. The app owns page
  hierarchy, layout HTML/CSS, identity and product-specific illustrations; a new
  hero, section sequence or grid is not automatically a missing DS component.
  Better owns visual quality and accessibility; this skill owns message and
  decision structure. Keep private component selectors and tokens intact.
  Propose a shared DS gap only for one reusable job with credible unrelated uses.
  Preserve useful BEUI anatomy, behavior and motion when adapting a shared control.
- Other specialist skills remain contextual: motion only when interaction is in
  scope; signup/onboarding only when those downstream flows are actually assigned.
  This skill has no required dependency on another product's documentation.
- Do not invent testimonials, customers, logos, scarcity, rankings, savings,
  guarantees or competitor claims. Do not create fear or shame to drive action.
- Present approved pricing, billing cadence, limits and trial requirements
  literally. Do not add tiers, remove free access, introduce a paywall, raise
  prices or change subscriptions because a heuristic favors them. Escalate
  missing or conflicting offer decisions to the product owner.
- Use the host product's language and brand. Do not impose a font, palette,
  section count, founder persona or third-party visual identity.

## Verify the result in scope

For a brief/copy review, return prioritized decisions, their rationale and any
unverified claims. Do not claim browser validation or conversion improvement.

For implementation, follow the host's UI gates and inspect the actual page on
desktop and narrow screens. Check reading order, keyboard/focus, contrast,
overflow and reduced motion where applicable. Follow the real CTA to the agreed
destination and inspect affected loading, error, success and return states; a
mock destination must stay explicit. Missing environments remain unverified.

For an authorized experiment, define the relevant behavioral metric and a guardrail
(for example, qualified signup/activation alongside abandonment or support burden).
If no baseline, traffic or analytics access exists, record a hypothesis and a
measurement plan, not a measured uplift. Do not add tracking, deploy or publish
without task authorization. Technical QA, aesthetic acceptance and demonstrated
conversion remain separate conclusions.
